About

Planned to
protect the land

Overton Retreat began with a simple idea: a peaceful, private place where nature, family and quality of life come first. Everything since, from the master plan to the rules on the lake, has been written to keep it that way for generations.

Portrait · Chip Webster
The architect

Chip Webster

Chip Webster Architecture, with offices in Nantucket, Boston and Vail, Colorado, drew the master plan and the architectural guidelines for Overton Retreat. A graduate of the M.I.T. School of Architecture, Webster has been featured in Forbes and other publications for his home designs.

His plan places every homesite to frame a bluff or lake view while keeping the woods around it, so the community reads as part of the mountain rather than something set on top of it.

The architecture

Three traditions, one mountain

The guidelines combine historic influences with modern forms. Curved or angled walls, generous glass and even towers are welcome, as long as the house belongs to the land around it.

Influence

Adirondack

Log and timber, deep porches and stone, the language of the great camps in the woods.

Influence

Rural Gothic

Steep gables, board and batten, and pointed windows that catch the light at the end of the day.

Influence

Tennessee vernacular

Simple farmhouse forms, metal roofs and wide porches built for summer storms and long evenings.

Award-winning

The pavilion

Crafted by Blue Ridge Timberwrights from hand-cut timbers and clad with a custom copper roof.
