Bluff view

The long view west

Set along the edge of Long Mountain with wooded privacy behind and the plateau falling away in front. These are the homesites for sunsets, wide windows and a deck that faces the weather.

Still · path down to the water
Lake view

Quiet water out front

Looking across one of two spring-fed lakes, close to the trail that loops the shoreline and a short walk from the pavilion. With electric motors only, the loudest thing on the water is a fish jumping.

Building at Overton

From homesite to home

Homes here follow guidelines written by Chip Webster Architecture, drawing on Adirondack, Rural Gothic and Tennessee vernacular forms. The result is a community that looks like it belongs to the mountain.

IChoose

Walk the land

Tour bluff and lake view homesites in person and find the one that fits how you want to live.

IIDesign

Design to the guidelines

Work with your architect within the Webster guidelines, which welcome glass, towers and curved walls alongside traditional forms.

IIIBuild

Build with care

Construction follows the community's contractor rules, which protect neighbors, trees and the roads you'll drive in on.

IVArrive

Pull through the gate

Friday evening, windows down. The property manager has looked after everything while you were gone.
